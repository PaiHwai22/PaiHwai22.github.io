Data Exploration with Palmer Penguins: Can physical measurements distinguish Penguin Species?

Author

Pai Hwai

Published

September 28, 2026

Introduction

Can we distinguish penguin species just by their physical measurements?

In this analysis, I explore the Palmer Penguins dataset to compare features such as body mass, flipper length, bill length, and bill depth. I investigate whether these measurements can help distinguish a penguin’s species and, if so, which feature appears to be the strongest indicator.

Data

Palmer Penguins, Palmer Station Antarctica LTER.

The dataset contains measurements for Adelie, Gentoo and Chinstrap penguins. The variables I explore are body mass, flipper length, bill length, and bill depth.

Data Cleaning

Removing missing values. Missing values can interfere with summary statistics and visualizations, so I remove observations with missing values in the measurements used in this analysis.

penguins_clean = penguins.dropna(
    subset=[
        "species",
        "body_mass_g",
        "flipper_length_mm",
        "bill_length_mm",
        "bill_depth_mm"
    ]
)

penguins_clean.shape
(342, 8)

Data Aggregation

To determine whether the penguin species differ physically, I compare the average body mass, flipper length, bill length, and bill depth for each species.

I do this using Python’s groupby() and agg() functions to group observations by species and calculate the mean of each physical measurement.

species_summary = (
    penguins_clean
    .groupby("species")
    .agg(
        mean_body_mass=("body_mass_g", "mean"),
        mean_flipper_length=("flipper_length_mm", "mean"),
        mean_bill_length=("bill_length_mm", "mean"),
        mean_bill_depth=("bill_depth_mm", "mean")
    )
    .round(1)
)

Comparing Variable Distributions

The averages show some differences between the three penguin species, but they do not show how much the individual measurements overlap.

To find which physical feature may be the strongest indicator of species, I compare the distribution of each measurement across the three species.

penguins_clean.boxplot(
    column="flipper_length_mm",
    by="species"
)

plt.xlabel("Species")
plt.ylabel("Flipper length (mm)")
plt.title("Flipper Length by Penguin Species")
plt.suptitle("")

plt.show()

The boxplot shows that Gentoo penguins generally have much longer flippers than Adelie and Chinstrap penguins. However, Adelie and Chinstrap have a lot of overlap in their flipper lengths.

This suggests that flipper length may be a useful indicator for identifying Gentoo penguins, but it may not be enough to clearly distinguish between all three species.

Recap of the Analysis

So far, each individual feature has some limitations:

  • Flipper length: helps identify Gentoo penguins.
  • Body mass: helps identify Gentoo penguins.
  • Bill length: helps identify Adelie penguins.
  • Bill depth: helps identify Gentoo penguins.

No single measurement clearly separates all three species, so I next look at whether combining two measurements provides a clearer distinction.

Combining Bill Length and Bill Depth

I compare bill length and bill depth together to see whether using two measurements provides a clearer distinction.

for species, group in penguins_clean.groupby("species"):
    plt.scatter(
        group["bill_length_mm"],
        group["bill_depth_mm"],
        label=species,
        alpha=0.7
    )

plt.xlabel("Bill length (mm)")
plt.ylabel("Bill depth (mm)")
plt.title("Bill Length vs. Bill Depth by Penguin Species")
plt.legend(title="Species")

plt.show()

The scatterplot shows a much clearer separation between the three species when bill length and bill depth are considered together.

Adelie penguins generally have shorter bills with greater bill depth, while Gentoo penguins tend to have longer but shallower bills. Chinstrap penguins generally have longer and deeper bills.

There is still some overlap between individual penguins, but using both measurements together distinguishes the three species more clearly than using either measurement alone.

Conclusion

Overall, the plots show that one measurement alone is not enough to clearly separate all three species. Gentoo penguins are easier to identify using body mass, flipper length, and bill depth, while bill length helps separate Adelie. The clearest result came from looking at bill length and bill depth together.

This data exploration suggests that looking at a combination of physical features is a better indicator of penguin species than relying on a single feature.
